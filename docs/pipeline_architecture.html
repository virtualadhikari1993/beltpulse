BeltPulse-Net pipeline

Finds foreign objects (wood, plastic, canga, jaspilito, tools) on an iron-ore belt. The belt's own motion lines up several photos of the same object. The models then combine those views, so one glared or blurred photo matters less.

trained (learns from labels) fixed physics or rule (no learning) data or frozen pretrained network

The whole pipeline

Steps 01–05 prepare data once. Step 06 trains the per-frame models on cached features, and steps 07, 08 and 10 use the trained models.

A real training sample from video 7_wood: target frame t = 2437, k = 12, so the views are frames 2425, 2437 and 2449. A wooden stick lies at x 784–1280, y 153–433 px, and the middle photo has painted glare.

The output has exactly the shape of the answer grid. Training compares the two square by square (the loss), and the test measures their overlap (IoU). Data: 2,716 training samples (980 with an object, 1,736 ore only, 2,016 with glare), 470 validation, 508 test, built from 8,068 distinct photo files.

Inside one per-frame model

Input: the cached features of the views plus how far the belt moved between them. Output: one probability per square of the middle photo t, a 22 × 40 grid where each square is 32 × 32 px of the original frame.

Alignment and attention, for one square

The belt carries the rock left, so it sits in a different place in each view. Sliding each view by its measured belt shift puts the rock on the same square. Attention then compares that square of view t with a small window around it in every view.

How the trust is computed

The saved model does not store "trust view t−k". It stores three learned matrices that turn any patch into a query, a key and a value. The trust weights are computed again for every square of every photo, from whatever the views contain there.

whatW_q, W_k, W_v: three 96 × 96 matrices (plus view and window-position embeddings)27 numbers per square that add up to 1

savedyes, in data/models/full/beltpulse.ptno; steps 07 and 08 only read them out to plot them
  1. Training never says "glare is bad". The model predicts with its current matrices.
  2. If the glared patch got a large weight, the blend looks washed out, the stick is missed, and the loss is high.
  3. The gradient says a lower score for glare-like keys would have lowered the loss, so W_q and W_k shift a little that way.
  4. After 2,716 samples × 30 epochs, glare-like patches get low scores, but only if that actually helped. The attention check in step 07 shows whether it happened: glared views clearly below 1/3 means it learned; about 1/3 means it is averaging.

What the baselines change

All six trained models share the same network and differ only in the fusion slot. The voting baselines are wired the other way round: each view decides alone first, and the answers are combined afterwards.

One decision per object

A sorter needs one decision per object, but an object is visible in 30 to 600 frames. Step 10 links per-frame detections into tracks using belt physics. A sequence transformer then reads each track and makes one call.

Run order

  1. 3 views, about 1 hour. Features for all samples (05), then the 6 models (06), evaluation with votes (07), several objects per photo (07b), attention pictures (08).
  2. 8 views, about 4 hours. The same steps with --variant T8w7.
  3. Per object. Step 10 with --detector full, using the full 3-view single model.
